# Five-domain scoring reference

Scores describe the documented behavior of one speaker toward another in a stated period. Always state the period boundaries. Score A toward B and B toward A separately, for each meaningful period and for the last documented period.

Each domain has four components scored 0 to 5. Domain score = sum of its four components (max 20). Total = sum of five domains (max 100).

## Anchors (same scale for all speakers)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Clearly opposite behavior, repeated across the documented opportunities |
| 1 | Mostly negative evidence, a little positive |
| 2 | Mixed, leaning negative |
| 3 | Mixed, leaning positive |
| 4 | Relatively consistent positive behavior with few significant exceptions |
| 5 | Consistent positive behavior across varied documented situations, including hard ones where documented |

These are judgment anchors built for this task. Do not present them as research norms. Justify each component with events, address contradictions, and avoid false precision.

No evidence, or no opportunity to show the behavior, is neither 0 nor a midpoint: write "cannot be assessed". If a component is missing, show a partial sum with its denominator (for example 11/15). Do not fill in or normalize to 100.

## Domains and components

**Reliability**
1. Consistency between versions of the same matter, allowing for justified corrections.
2. Keeping commitments that have documented follow-up.
3. Transparency about limits and changes.
4. Acknowledging mistakes and correcting information.
Distinguish a promise, a self-report of performance, and the other side's confirmation of performance. Consistency is not proof of truth.

**Friendship**
1. Initiative toward shared contact.
2. Reciprocity in maintaining the connection.
3. Sharing and shared experiences that have evidence.
4. Respectful treatment even during disagreement.
Adjust to the relationship type; do not expect romantic language in a friendly or professional relationship.

**Sacrifice (state which definition in the heading; use only the confirmed one)**
- Definition (a), investment and giving up: time and effort when needed; reasonable flexibility for the other; a gesture with documented personal cost; willingness to compromise mutually. Do not reward self-harm, coerced appeasement, or giving up boundaries. Do not assume a cost that was not documented.
- Definition (b), emotional closeness: personal sharing; expressing vulnerability; receiving the other's sharing; mutual expressions of connection and understanding.
Never mix the two and never add a sixth domain.

**Support**
1. Acknowledging an emotion or difficulty the other expressed.
2. Practical help matched to the request.
3. Follow-up after a request for help or distress.
4. Encouraging the other's goals while respecting their choices.

**Caring**
1. Self-initiated interest in the other's wellbeing.
2. Remembering details that matter to them.
3. Adapting to needs and preferences they expressed.
4. Considering their boundaries and wellbeing in decisions.
Distinguish routine caring from support given in response to a specific need.

## Presenting a score

For every domain show: the four component scores, the explanation, evidence references (message ids), a verbal confidence level (low, medium, high), and overlap notes. One event can bear on several domains but it is not several independent pieces of evidence.

An optional whole-archive summary is allowed only with adequate basis in all five domains. Apply the same anchors, explain how periods were weighted, never take an automatic average, and never let a high-volume period decide just by its volume. Do not hide a serious event inside a high total.
